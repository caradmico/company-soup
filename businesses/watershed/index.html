Netarts Bay — Habitat Study & Limiting Factors

Demeter Design, 2008 · Tillamook Estuaries Partnership / OWEB

  • Netarts Bay is a saline-dominated estuary (~2,000 acres) draining a ~14,000-acre watershed — estuary, dune, and salmon streams packed into a small area.
  • Ten streams surveyed with ODFW AQI plus a modified limiting-factors protocol (Whiskey, Jackson complex, O’Hara, Austin, Crown Zellarbach, Fall, Rice, Yeager, Lower Northbay, Hodgdon).
  • Primary Chum limit: lack of well-sorted spawning gravels (often tied to low wood). Gravels abundant and volcanic but rarely well sorted and often highly embedded.
  • Netarts is the southernmost remaining extent of Chum range. Whiskey Creek Chum spawners fell from nearly 1,500 (1973) to ~2–4 in recent years; Coho escapees ~5.
  • Coho limited by poor/absent spawning habitat and equivalently by poor summer rearing (not temperature-limited in measured reaches).
  • Restoration priority: Whiskey & Jackson. Chum also needs barrier removal (Whiskey hatchery diversion; first O’Hara culvert). Few absolute barriers elsewhere; many non-timber culverts undersized/failing.

Upper Nestucca — Sediment, Shade & Habitat Complexity

Demeter Design LLC · Mico, C. & Mico, L. · March 2007 · BLM contract #HAP064172

  • Characterizes ambient water quality and physical habitat in the Upper Nestucca network for BLM Clean Water Act DMA obligations (GRTS design; ~80 sites on 3rd-order+ BLM streams).
  • None of 33 sites exceeded 20% SAFN in riffles/glides — the 303(d) listing was judged no longer applicable for current BLM conditions in this study.
  • Erodible lithologies carry significantly higher fines than resistant reaches.
  • Properly sized large-crossing culverts do not appear to add excess fines relative to the network.
  • Supports WQRPs, NEPA baselines, and management-effect evaluation (LRBS / %SAFN relationships; sample frame ~804 km).

Tillamook Bay — Data Synthesis & CERP Tool

Demeter Design, 2008 · Tillamook Bay Watershed Council / OWEB

  • After Coastal Coho Assessment labeled Tillamook Bay Coho non-viable, partners needed a shared prioritization method for outreach, restoration, and conservation.
  • CERP spreadsheet tool flexibly ranks 94 seventh-field HUCs from existing habitat, biotic, GIS, and written data — updateable when new data arrives.
  • ODFW analysis in the synthesis: essentially the entire TBW has poor Coho habitat — “restoration needed nearly everywhere.”
  • AQI coverage incomplete — 12 sub-basins with no data at the time of the synthesis.
  • Deliverables: priority basins, CERP tool, maps, 7th-field tables, 5th-field summaries — built to favor biotic importance over opportunistic picks.
